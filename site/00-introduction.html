Start a small podcast, calmly

This is a calm, plain-English manual for starting a small podcast or audio show — written for authors and one-person LLCs who already have a full life to run.

You will not find "buy this $600 microphone" anywhere in here. The guide is built on one stubborn idea, repeated in every chapter:

Decide what you will say, record in your bedroom, edit and host with free tools, and only spend money once the show has earned it.

Who this is for

  • Authors (self-published or trade) who want a low-cost way to talk to readers and other writers.
  • One-person LLC founders who want an audio show as part of the business, not a second full-time job.
  • Anyone who has been told they need a studio, a co-host, and a $900 microphone before they're allowed to have opinions on the internet. You do not.

If you record spoken audio for a living — audiobooks, narration — you are also in the right place, but you want the sibling project, the audiobook-recording handbook, which covers performance, breath, and narration craft in depth. This guide covers the show around the recording.

Everything here follows the same approach as The Massive AI Manual and everything else published by Asdale Dynamics, LLC:

  • Plain English, step by step, like a friend would explain it — never condescending.
  • Free or cheap first. Local-first tools where sensible.
  • Tested. The build scripts and tests on this site are real; nothing here is vibes-only.
  • No hype, no pressure, no//@shiny-object chasing.

How to use this guide

  1. Read What to say before you buy anything. Do not buy anything until you finish it.
  2. Record your first episode in your bedroom exactly as described in Bedroom recording on a budget.
  3. Edit and publish with the free tools compared in Free editing and hosting, compared.
  4. Use the episode skeleton in An episode structure you can sustain so every episode files itself.
  5. When you want transcripts and show notes, follow AI transcripts and show notes, with verification — AI drafts, human verifies.
  6. If you have guests, read Guest etiquette, from invite to thank-you first. It will save you embarrassment and re-records.
  7. Consistency without burnout is the chapter that keeps the show alive in month four.
  8. If the show stops being fun, Quitting politely shows you how to end it like a professional, with your dignity and your listeners intact.

Along the way you will fill out four worksheets (all linked from the index page), and you can look up any jargon in the glossary. If a word in this guide confuses you, that is a bug in the guide — check the glossary, and the word's entry will explain it like a friend would.

What this guide is not

  • Not a course on monetization, sponsors, or "7-figure podcast secrets." None of that is needed to start.
  • Not a substitute for legal or tax advice about your LLC. It tells you what to stay away from (guest releases, copyright basics) in plain terms, then points you to check your own situation.
  • Not a gear review blog.

The whole guide in six sentences

Say what you'll say before you spend money. Record in the quietest room you own with whatever you already have. Edit with free software, host on a free tier of a real host, and keep your RSS feed. Build an episode shape you can repeat tired. Let AI draft transcripts and show notes, but verify every name, number, and quote yourself. Keep a gentle pace — and quit politely if it stops being fun.
