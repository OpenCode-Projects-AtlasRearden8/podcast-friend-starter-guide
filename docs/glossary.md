# Glossary

Plain-English definitions of every piece of jargon this guide uses. Written like a friend would explain it — if a term here is still confusing after reading, the confusion is a bug in the glossary. Entries are alphabetical.

- **Audacity, GarageBand, Reaper** — Editing software (the "DAW" category). Compared in [Chapter 3](03-free-editing-and-hosting-compared.md).
- **Bitrate (and MP3 vs WAV)** — WAV is the raw, large, pristine master; MP3 is the small, published file, and *bitrate* is how much data per second it carries. Record WAV, publish spoken-word MP3 at 128 kbps or better — bigger numbers stop helping long before you'd notice (see [Chapter 2](02-bedroom-recording-on-a-budget.md)).
- **Body doubling** — Working alongside another person (even silently, even over a call) so starting is easier. A common low-energy-productivity tool, named in [Chapter 7](07-consistency-without-burnout.md).
- **Buzzsprout, Podbean, RSS.com, Spotify for Creators** — Examples of podcast *hosts*: companies that store your audio and produce your RSS feed. Compared in [Chapter 3](03-free-editing-and-hosting-compared.md).
- **Call to action (CTA)** — The *one thing* you ask listeners to do after an episode. One, not five (see [Chapter 4](04-episode-structure-you-can-sustain.md)).
- **Chapter markers** — Timestamps inside an episode file that create a table of contents in the listener's app. Nice-to-have; show-notes timestamps already cover it (see [Chapter 5](05-ai-transcripts-and-show-notes-with-verification.md)).
- **Cold open** — Starting an episode with the most interesting sentence, before any welcome or music. Fifteen to forty-five seconds (see [Chapter 4](04-episode-structure-you-can-sustain.md)).
- **Condenser vs dynamic microphone** — A condenser hears *everything* (echo, fridges, your thoughts); a dynamic hears *you*, mostly. Bedrooms want dynamic (see [Chapter 2](02-bedroom-recording-on-a-budget.md)).
- **DAW** — Digital Audio Workstation: the editing-software category (Audacity, GarageBand, Reaper). Your DAW is where mistakes get cut out (see [Chapter 3](03-free-editing-and-hosting-compared.md)).
- **dBFS, the -12 to -6 dB zone** — How loud your recording meter says you are. Peaks landing between minus twelve and minus six, never touching red, means clean headroom and no clipping (see [Chapter 2](02-bedroom-recording-on-a-budget.md)).
- **Feed URL / RSS feed** — The web address of your show's "mailing list." Apps (Apple Podcasts, Spotify, Pocket Casts and friends) read it to fetch new episodes automatically. Who owns this address matters more than who stores the files ([Chapter 3](03-free-editing-and-hosting-compared.md)).
- **Gain vs volume** — Gain is the level going *into* the recorder; volume is the level going *into your ears*. Recording problems are fixed with gain or distance, never by turning computer volume up ([Chapter 2](02-bedroom-recording-on-a-budget.md)).
- **Hosting (podcast)** — The service holding your MP3s and producing the RSS feed. Free tiers exist and are fine to start on — read the comparison and the fine print in [Chapter 3](03-free-editing-and-hosting-compared.md).
- **ID3 tags** — The metadata written inside an MP3 (title, artist, artwork). Your host writes these for you; there is no reason to hand-edit them (see [Chapter 3](03-free-editing-and-hosting-compared.md)).
- **LUFS, -16 LUFS** — Loudness Units, the standard for *perceived* loudness. Voice-first podcasts target around -16; one menu in Audacity ("Loudness Normalization") gets you there, or your host applies it (technique in [Chapter 2](02-bedroom-recording-on-a-budget.md), settings in [Chapter 3](03-free-editing-and-hosting-compared.md)).
- **Minimum viable episode (MVE)** — A ten-minute, one-segment, one-idea episode that keeps the show's heartbeat alive on a bad week. Defined in [Chapter 4](04-episode-structure-you-can-sustain.md).
- **Mono vs stereo** — Voice-first shows are mono: smaller files, identical on every playback device. Stereo belongs to music-produced shows, not your bottleneck (see [Chapter 2](02-bedroom-recording-on-a-budget.md)).
- **Noise floor** — The steady background hiss or hum that exists even when you sit silent. Lower it before you spend money: unplug the fridge, close the window, record when the world is quiet (see [Chapter 2](02-bedroom-recording-on-a-budget.md)).
- **Normalization (loudness)** — Making every episode land at a consistent perceived loudness so listeners don't ride the volume knob between shows. One click in Audacity, or handled by your host ([Chapter 3](03-free-editing-and-hosting-compared.md)).
- **Podfade** — The slow, unannounced death of a show: weekly becomes monthly becomes silence. Avoided with the deliberate exit in [Chapter 8](08-quitting-politely.md).
- **Pop filter** — A screen that stops plosive blasts. A mic held 4–8 inches away, slightly off-axis from your mouth, does most of that job for free ([Chapter 2](02-bedroom-recording-on-a-budget.md)).
- **Plosive** — The little wind blast your mouth makes on "p" and "b" — sounds like a tiny boom. Sideways mic placement beats them (see [Chapter 2](02-bedroom-recording-on-a-budget.md)).
- **Room tone** — Ten seconds of your room being silent, recorded at the top of a session. Editing software uses it to patch gaps so cuts sound like nothing happened (see [Chapter 2](02-bedroom-recording-on-a-budget.md)).
- **RSS (Really Simple Syndication)** — The old, sturdy format that lets apps automatically fetch new episodes. RSS is why podcast subscriptions are meaningfully *yours* rather than a platform's (see [Chapter 3](03-free-editing-and-hosting-compared.md)).
- **Season** — A defined run of episodes (8–12 is a common shape) with a planned end. Endings in sight are the strongest anti-burnout device in [Chapter 7](07-consistency-without-burnout.md).
- **Show notes** — The written companion to an episode: promise, what-you'll-learn bullets, timestamps, links, one action. Template in [Chapter 5](05-ai-transcripts-and-show-notes-with-verification.md).
- **Sponsorship / dynamic ad insertion** — The money mechanisms. Both require an audience you do not have at episode 1, so both live in the "later" folder; nothing in this guide needs them (see [Chapter 3](03-free-editing-and-hosting-compared.md)).
- **Timestamps** — Minute markers in show notes (01:12 The room). Gold for listeners and for your own archive searches; AI drafts them, you spot-check them ([Chapter 5](05-ai-transcripts-and-show-notes-with-verification.md)).
- **Transcript** — The written text of an episode. AI drafts it, you verify it ([Chapter 5](05-ai-transcripts-and-show-notes-with-verification.md)); it doubles as a searchable archive of your own thinking.
- **Whisper / whisper.cpp / faster-whisper** — Open-source speech-to-text you can run locally — free, private, no upload. The local-first transcription option in [Chapter 5](05-ai-transcripts-and-show-notes-with-verification.md).
- **XLR vs USB** — XLR is the professional cable standard that needs an interface; USB microphones plug straight into a computer. Budget hierarchy for bedrooms: USB first (see [Chapter 2](02-bedroom-recording-on-a-budget.md)).
- **Zoom, Riverside, SquadCast** — Remote-recording platforms that capture each speaker as a separate local file at better quality than a live call. Useful for interviews in [Chapter 6](06-guest-etiquette.md); most have free tiers with limits.
